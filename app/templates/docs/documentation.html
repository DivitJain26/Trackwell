About TrackWell

TrackWell is a final-year B.Tech Computer Science & Engineering project at MIT School of Computing, MIT-ADT University, Pune, developed by Arya Phansalkar and Divit Jain (Group BCC21) under the guidance of Prof. Ujjwala Nivas Salunkhe.

It helps faculty identify students who may be academically at risk before final examinations by collecting academic and behavioural indicators, predicting a Low / Medium / High risk category with a scikit-learn model, explaining the main contributing factors, raising alerts, and tracking interventions. Everything runs locally: there are no external APIs or cloud services.

Workflow
  1. Register students through the form or by importing a CSV (Student management / CSV import). Each student ID must be unique.
  2. Record assessments on a student's profile to enable the score-trend feature (needs at least two scores).
  3. Train the model (Model status page or python scripts/train_model.py). Training compares three algorithms and stores a versioned artifact with its evaluation report.
  4. Generate predictions for all students, one class, or only students without a prediction. New High Risk cases and risk increases create alerts.
  5. Review alerts and the student's context, then record interventions with follow-up dates and outcomes.
  6. Students sign in to see their own indicators, observations, suggested actions and (if enabled in Settings) their risk category.
Interpreting predictions

Risk category. The model outputs a probability for Low, Medium and High. The category shown is the class with the highest probability. The risk score (0-100) is 100 × (0.5 × P(Medium) + P(High)) and is used for ranking only.

Model Model-derived factors are computed by replacing one indicator at a time with the training-set typical value (median or mode) and measuring the change in the risk score. They reflect what the trained model actually responds to for this student.

Descriptive Observations compare a student's indicators with reference levels configured by the administrator (for example attendance below 75%). They are plain rules and are deliberately labelled separately so they are never mistaken for model output.

Limitations. The bundled training data is synthetic. Metrics obtained on it show that the pipeline works but say nothing about real students. Predictions are decision-support signals and must always be reviewed by faculty before acting. Intervention outcomes are not fed back into training automatically; retraining requires a documented labelled dataset and an explicit training action.

Target definition and model selection

Each training row contains the indicators available before final exams plus final_exam_pct, from which the label is derived: High < 40%, Medium 40-55%, Low ≥ 55%. The exam result and the label are never used as inputs, which prevents target leakage.

Candidates: Logistic Regression, Random Forest, Gradient Boosting. Each is evaluated with stratified 5-fold cross-validation on the 80% training split and then on the 20% held-out split. The model with the highest 0.5 × macro F1 + 0.5 × recall(High) on the held-out split is selected and refitted on all labelled rows. Preprocessing (median/most-frequent imputation, scaling, one-hot encoding) is part of the pipeline and is fitted only on training data.

Artifacts are stored in instance/models/ with a SHA-256 checksum recorded in the database. The application refuses to load an artifact whose checksum does not match, so arbitrary pickle files cannot be executed.

Roles

Admin – manages user accounts, settings, model activation, plus everything faculty can do.

Faculty – manages students, imports data, runs predictions, reviews alerts, records interventions, trains models.

Student – sees only their own record. Faculty notes and other students' data are never shown.

Operating the system

Start: run run.bat (Windows) or ./run.sh (macOS/Linux), then open http://127.0.0.1:5000.

First admin: the login page offers a one-time setup form while no administrator account exists, or run python scripts/create_admin.py.

Demo data: python scripts/seed_demo.py imports the synthetic students and creates demo faculty/student accounts that must change their password at first login.

Backup: python scripts/backup_db.py copies the database to instance/backups/.

Reset: stop the app and delete instance/trackwell.db.

Tests: python -m pytest.

Security notes
  • Passwords are stored as salted hashes (Werkzeug scrypt).
  • Every state-changing form carries a CSRF token.
  • Authorisation is enforced on the server for every route.
  • Session cookies are HttpOnly and SameSite=Lax.
  • Uploads are limited to 5 MB CSV files and parsed with pandas; nothing is executed.
  • Logs in instance/logs/ never contain passwords.
